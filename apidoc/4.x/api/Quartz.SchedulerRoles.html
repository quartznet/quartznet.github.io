Remarks

A role is the application's: a ClaimsPrincipal.IsInRole answer, whatever issued the claim. A caller in Administrators may do everything, one in Operators may view and operate, and one in Viewers may view. A caller in none of them may do nothing at all, reads included, so a role that should only look is still named here.

Which operation needs which level is the route table's, and Operations moves an operation that changes something to the other level. Triggering a job with job data stays at Administer whatever Operations says about TriggerJob: a stored NativeJob reads the command it runs from its job data.

Remarks

Only an operation that changes something may be named, and only at Operate or Administer: a read is every role's, and a change is never a viewer's. A name the HTTP API does not publish is refused when the policy is built, since a misspelt override would otherwise grant or withhold nothing in silence.

Operations["AddCalendar"] = SchedulerAccess.Operate lets operators maintain calendars; Operations["InterruptJob"] = SchedulerAccess.Administer keeps interrupting to administrators.
